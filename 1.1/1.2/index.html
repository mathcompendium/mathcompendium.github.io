Try It 1.2

Your turn: problems like the one in the video
  1. Find the prime factorization of $80$.
  2. Find the prime factorization of $60$.
Show a hint
Start with any factor pair and keep splitting each factor that is not prime, as in a factor tree. Write the primes in order from smallest to largest.
Show the answers

  1. $2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 5$

    $80 = 8 \cdot 10 = (2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2)(2 \cdot 5)$.

  2. $2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$

    $60 = 6 \cdot 10 = (2 \cdot 3)(2 \cdot 5)$.

Show the worked solution

The same steps as the video. Each ▶ button plays the part of the video where that step is explained.

Problem 1

$80$Start with any factor pair.
$=$$8 \cdot 10$$80 = 8 \cdot 10$. Neither factor is prime, so both branches keep going.
$=$$2 \cdot 4 \cdot 2 \cdot 5$$8 = 2 \cdot 4$ and $10 = 2 \cdot 5$. Circle the primes: $2$, $2$ and $5$.
$=$$2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 5$$4 = 2 \cdot 2$. Every factor is now prime; write them from smallest to largest.
Check: $2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 16$, and $16 \cdot 5 = 80$.
Problem 2

Try problem 2 again on your own first, using problem 1 as your guide. Then compare.

$60$Start with any factor pair.
$=$$6 \cdot 10$$60 = 6 \cdot 10$. Neither factor is prime.
$=$$2 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 5$$6 = 2 \cdot 3$ and $10 = 2 \cdot 5$. All four factors are prime.
$=$$2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$Write the primes from smallest to largest.
Check: $2 \cdot 2 = 4$, $4 \cdot 3 = 12$, and $12 \cdot 5 = 60$.
