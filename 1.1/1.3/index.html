Find the Least Common Multiple Using the Prime Factors Method

Try It 1.3

Your turn: problems like the one in the video
  1. Find the LCM of $9$ and $12$ using the prime factors method.
  2. Find the LCM of $18$ and $24$ using the prime factors method.
Show a hint
Write each number as a product of primes, lining up matching primes in columns. Bring down one prime from each column and multiply.
Show the answers

  1. $36$

    $9 = 3 \cdot 3$ and $12 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3$, so the LCM is $2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 = 36$.

  2. $72$

    $18 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 3$ and $24 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3$, so the LCM is $2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 = 72$.

Show the worked solution

The same steps as the video. Each ▶ button plays the part of the video where that step is explained.

Problem 1

$9$$=$$3 \cdot 3$Write each number as a product of primes.
$12$$=$$2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3$
Line up matching primes in columns. Only $12$ has $2$s, so they get two columns. Both numbers have a $3$, so those share a column, and $9$'s second $3$ gets one more. That makes four columns: $2$, $2$, $3$, $3$.
$2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 3$$=$$36$Bring down one prime from each column and multiply. The LCM is $36$.
Check: $36 \div 9 = 4$ and $36 \div 12 = 3$, so $36$ is a multiple of both.
Problem 2

Try problem 2 again on your own first, using problem 1 as your guide. Then compare.

$18$$=$$2 \cdot 3 \cdot 3$Write each number as a product of primes.
$24$$=$$2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3$
Line up matching primes in columns. Both have a $2$ and a $3$. $24$ has two more $2$s, and $18$ has a second $3$. That makes five columns: $2$, $2$, $2$, $3$, $3$.
$2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 3$$=$$72$Bring down one prime from each column and multiply. The LCM is $72$.
Check: $72 \div 18 = 4$ and $72 \div 24 = 3$.
