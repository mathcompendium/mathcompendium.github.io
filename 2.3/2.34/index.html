Use the Pythagorean Theorem to Find a Leg

Try It 2.34

Your turn: problems like the one in the video
  1. Use the Pythagorean Theorem to find the length of the leg $b$ of a right triangle whose other leg is $15$ and whose hypotenuse is $17$.
  2. Use the Pythagorean Theorem to find the length of the leg $b$ of a right triangle whose other leg is $9$ and whose hypotenuse is $15$.
Show a hint
Put the hypotenuse, the side across from the right angle, in for $c$. Subtract to get $b^2$ alone, then take the positive square root.
Show the answers

  1. The length of the leg is $8$.

  2. The length of the leg is $12$.

Show the worked solution

Problem 1

The hypotenuse is the side across from the right angle, $17$. The other leg is $15$. Let $b$ = the unknown leg.
$a^2 + b^2$$=$$c^2$The Pythagorean Theorem: $a$ and $b$ are the legs, $c$ is the hypotenuse.
$15^2 + b^2$$=$$17^2$Substitute the leg $15$ and the hypotenuse $17$.
$225 + b^2$$=$$289$Simplify the squares.
$225 \hl{{} - 225} + b^2$$=$$289 \hl{{} - 225}$Subtract $225$ from both sides.
$b^2$$=$$64$Simplify.
$b$$=$$\sqrt{64}$Use the definition of square root. A length can't be negative, so use the positive square root.
$b$$=$$8$Simplify.
The length of the leg is $8$.

Check: $15^2 + 8^2 = 225 + 64 = 289$, which is $17^2$.

Problem 2

Try problem 2 again on your own first, using problem 1 as your guide. Then compare.

The hypotenuse is the side across from the right angle, $15$. The other leg is $9$. Let $b$ = the unknown leg.
$a^2 + b^2$$=$$c^2$The Pythagorean Theorem: $a$ and $b$ are the legs, $c$ is the hypotenuse.
$9^2 + b^2$$=$$15^2$Substitute the leg $9$ and the hypotenuse $15$.
$81 + b^2$$=$$225$Simplify the squares.
$81 \hl{{} - 81} + b^2$$=$$225 \hl{{} - 81}$Subtract $81$ from both sides.
$b^2$$=$$144$Simplify.
$b$$=$$\sqrt{144}$Use the definition of square root. A length can't be negative, so use the positive square root.
$b$$=$$12$Simplify.
The length of the leg is $12$.

Check: $9^2 + 12^2 = 81 + 144 = 225$, which is $15^2$.
