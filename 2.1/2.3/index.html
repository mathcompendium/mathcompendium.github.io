Problem 1

$\dfrac{1}{3}(6u + 3)$$=$$7 - u$The problem. The right side is already simple.
$2u + 1$$=$$7 - u$Distribute $\frac{1}{3}$: $\frac{1}{3} \cdot 6u = 2u$ and $\frac{1}{3} \cdot 3 = 1$.
$2u + 1 \hl{{} + u}$$=$$7 - u \hl{{} + u}$There is a $-u$ on the right, so add $u$ to both sides.
$3u + 1$$=$$7$Combine like terms.
$3u + 1 \hl{{} - 1}$$=$$7 \hl{{} - 1}$To undo adding $1$, subtract $1$ from both sides.
$3u$$=$$6$Simplify.
$\dfrac{3u}{\hl{3}}$$=$$\dfrac{6}{\hl{3}}$Divide both sides by $3$.
$u$$=$$2$Simplify.

Check: put $2$ in for $u$. The left side is $\frac{1}{3}(12 + 3) = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 15 = 5$, and the right side is $7 - 2 = 5$.

Problem 2

Try problem 2 again on your own first, using problem 1 as your guide. Then compare.

$\dfrac{2}{3}(9x - 12)$$=$$8 + 2x$The problem. The right side is already simple.
$6x - 8$$=$$8 + 2x$Distribute $\frac{2}{3}$: $\frac{2}{3} \cdot 9x = 6x$ and $\frac{2}{3} \cdot 12 = 8$.
$6x - 8 \hl{{} - 2x}$$=$$8 + 2x \hl{{} - 2x}$$6$ is bigger than $2$, so keep the $x$ terms on the left: subtract $2x$ from both sides.
$4x - 8$$=$$8$Combine like terms.
$4x - 8 \hl{{} + 8}$$=$$8 \hl{{} + 8}$To undo subtracting $8$, add $8$ to both sides.
$4x$$=$$16$Simplify.
$\dfrac{4x}{\hl{4}}$$=$$\dfrac{16}{\hl{4}}$Divide both sides by $4$.
$x$$=$$4$Simplify.

Check: put $4$ in for $x$. The left side is $\frac{2}{3}(36 - 12) = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 24 = 16$, and the right side is $8 + 8 = 16$.
