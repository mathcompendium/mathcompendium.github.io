Try It 2.7

Your turn: problems like the one in the video
  1. Classify the equation as a conditional equation, an identity, or a contradiction, and then state the solution: $11(q + 3) - 5 = 19$.
  2. Classify the equation as a conditional equation, an identity, or a contradiction, and then state the solution: $6 + 14(k - 8) = 95$.
Show a hint
Solve the usual way. If the variable stays and you end with one value, the equation is conditional.
Show the answers

  1. conditional equation; $q = -\dfrac{9}{11}$

  2. conditional equation; $k = \dfrac{201}{14}$

Show the worked solution

The same steps as the video. Each ▶ button plays the part of the video where that step is explained.

Problem 1

$11(q + 3) - 5$$=$$19$The problem.
$11q + 33 - 5$$=$$19$Distribute the $11$. The $-5$ comes down.
$11q + 28$$=$$19$Combine like terms.
$11q + 28 \hl{{} - 28}$$=$$19 \hl{{} - 28}$To undo adding $28$, subtract $28$ from both sides.
$11q$$=$$-9$Simplify.
$\dfrac{11q}{\hl{11}}$$=$$\dfrac{-9}{\hl{11}}$Divide both sides by $11$.
$q$$=$$-\dfrac{9}{11}$Simplify.
The variable did not drop out, and there is one value, so the equation is conditional.

Check: put $-\frac{9}{11}$ in for $q$. $-\frac{9}{11} + 3 = \frac{24}{11}$, and $11 \cdot \frac{24}{11} - 5 = 24 - 5 = 19$.

Problem 2

Try problem 2 again on your own first, using problem 1 as your guide. Then compare.

$6 + 14(k - 8)$$=$$95$The problem.
$6 + 14k - 112$$=$$95$Distribute the $14$. The $6$ comes down.
$14k - 106$$=$$95$Combine like terms.
$14k - 106 \hl{{} + 106}$$=$$95 \hl{{} + 106}$To undo subtracting $106$, add $106$ to both sides.
$14k$$=$$201$Simplify.
$\dfrac{14k}{\hl{14}}$$=$$\dfrac{201}{\hl{14}}$Divide both sides by $14$.
$k$$=$$\dfrac{201}{14}$Simplify. $201$ and $14$ share no factor, so the fraction is in lowest terms.
The variable did not drop out, and there is one value, so the equation is conditional.

Check: put $\frac{201}{14}$ in for $k$. $\frac{201}{14} - 8 = \frac{89}{14}$, and $6 + 14 \cdot \frac{89}{14} = 6 + 89 = 95$.
