  1. Solve: $7 = \frac{1}{2}x + \frac{3}{4}x - \frac{2}{3}x$.
  2. Solve: $-1 = \frac{1}{2}u + \frac{1}{4}u - \frac{2}{3}u$.
Show a hint
Find the LCD of all three fractions and multiply both sides by it. Then combine like terms.
Show the answers

  1. $x = 12$

  2. $u = -12$

Show the worked solution

The same steps as the video. Each ▶ button plays the part of the video where that step is explained.

Problem 1

$7$$=$$\dfrac{1}{2}x + \dfrac{3}{4}x - \dfrac{2}{3}x$The problem. The denominators are $2$, $4$ and $3$, so the LCD is $12$.
$\hl{12}(7)$$=$$\hl{12}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}x + \dfrac{3}{4}x - \dfrac{2}{3}x\right)$Multiply both sides by the LCD, $12$.
$84$$=$$6x + 9x - 8x$Distribute: $12 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 6$, $12 \cdot \frac{3}{4} = 9$ and $12 \cdot \frac{2}{3} = 8$.
$84$$=$$7x$Combine like terms: $6 + 9 - 8 = 7$.
$\dfrac{84}{\hl{7}}$$=$$\dfrac{7x}{\hl{7}}$Divide both sides by $7$.
$12$$=$$x$Simplify.

Check: put $12$ in for $x$. $6 + 9 - 8 = 7$, which matches the left side.

Problem 2

Try problem 2 again on your own first, using problem 1 as your guide. Then compare.

$-1$$=$$\dfrac{1}{2}u + \dfrac{1}{4}u - \dfrac{2}{3}u$The problem. The denominators are $2$, $4$ and $3$, so the LCD is $12$.
$\hl{12}(-1)$$=$$\hl{12}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}u + \dfrac{1}{4}u - \dfrac{2}{3}u\right)$Multiply both sides by the LCD, $12$.
$-12$$=$$6u + 3u - 8u$Distribute: $12 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 6$, $12 \cdot \frac{1}{4} = 3$ and $12 \cdot \frac{2}{3} = 8$.
$-12$$=$$u$Combine like terms: $6 + 3 - 8 = 1$, so the right side is just $u$.

Check: put $-12$ in for $u$. $-6 - 3 + 8 = -1$, which matches the left side.
