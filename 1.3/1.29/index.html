Problem 1

ⓐ $\dfrac{3a}{4} - \dfrac{8}{9}$
$\dfrac{3a}{4} - \dfrac{8}{9}$The operation is subtraction, so we need a common denominator. $4 = 2 \cdot 2$ and $9 = 3 \cdot 3$, so the LCD is $36$.
$=$$\dfrac{3a \cdot 9}{4 \cdot 9} - \dfrac{8 \cdot 4}{9 \cdot 4}$$4$ is missing $9$, and $9$ is missing $4$.
$=$$\dfrac{27a}{36} - \dfrac{32}{36}$Multiply.
$=$$\dfrac{27a - 32}{36}$Subtract the numerators. Careful: $27a$ and $32$ are not like terms, so they can't be combined.
ⓑ $\dfrac{3a}{4} \cdot \dfrac{8}{9}$
$\dfrac{3a}{4} \cdot \dfrac{8}{9}$The operation is multiplication, so no common denominator is needed.
$=$$\dfrac{3a \cdot 8}{4 \cdot 9}$Multiply the numerators and the denominators.
$=$$\dfrac{\cancel{3} \cdot a \cdot 2 \cdot \cancel{4}}{\cancel{4} \cdot \cancel{3} \cdot 3}$$8 = 2 \cdot 4$ and $9 = 3 \cdot 3$. Divide out the $3$ and the $4$.
$=$$\dfrac{2a}{3}$
Problem 2

Try problem 2 again on your own first, using problem 1 as your guide. Then compare.

ⓐ $\dfrac{4k}{5} - \dfrac{1}{6}$
$\dfrac{4k}{5} - \dfrac{1}{6}$Subtraction needs a common denominator. $5$ and $6 = 2 \cdot 3$ share no primes, so the LCD is $30$.
$=$$\dfrac{4k \cdot 6}{5 \cdot 6} - \dfrac{1 \cdot 5}{6 \cdot 5}$$5$ is missing $6$, and $6$ is missing $5$.
$=$$\dfrac{24k}{30} - \dfrac{5}{30}$Multiply.
$=$$\dfrac{24k - 5}{30}$Subtract the numerators. $24k$ and $5$ are not like terms.
ⓑ $\dfrac{4k}{5} \cdot \dfrac{1}{6}$
$\dfrac{4k}{5} \cdot \dfrac{1}{6}$Multiplication: no common denominator is needed.
$=$$\dfrac{4k \cdot 1}{5 \cdot 6}$Multiply the numerators and the denominators.
$=$$\dfrac{\cancel{2} \cdot 2k}{5 \cdot \cancel{2} \cdot 3}$$4 = 2 \cdot 2$ and $6 = 2 \cdot 3$. Divide out a $2$.
$=$$\dfrac{2k}{15}$
