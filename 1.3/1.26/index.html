Find the Quotient of Two Negative Fractions

Previous example · Example 1.25Multiply a Negative Fraction and a Negative TermNext example · Example 1.27Simplify a Complex Fraction with Variables

Try It 1.26

Your turn: problems like the one in the video
  1. Divide: $-\dfrac{7}{27} \div \left(-\dfrac{35}{36}\right)$.
  2. Divide: $-\dfrac{5}{14} \div \left(-\dfrac{15}{28}\right)$.
Show a hint
Multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction. Both are negative, so the quotient is positive.
Show the answers

  1. $\dfrac{4}{15}$

    $\dfrac{7}{27} \cdot \dfrac{36}{35} = \dfrac{7 \cdot 4 \cdot 9}{9 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7} = \dfrac{4}{15}$.

  2. $\dfrac{2}{3}$

    $\dfrac{5}{14} \cdot \dfrac{28}{15} = \dfrac{5 \cdot 2 \cdot 14}{14 \cdot 3 \cdot 5} = \dfrac{2}{3}$.

Show the worked solution

The same steps as the video. Each ▶ button plays the part of the video where that step is explained.

Problem 1

$-\dfrac{7}{27} \div \left(-\dfrac{35}{36}\right)$The problem.
$=$$-\dfrac{7}{27} \cdot \left(-\dfrac{36}{35}\right)$Multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction.
$=$$\dfrac{7}{27} \cdot \dfrac{36}{35}$Both are negative, so the product is positive.
$=$$\dfrac{7 \cdot 36}{27 \cdot 35}$Multiply the numerators and the denominators.
$=$$\dfrac{\cancel{7} \cdot 4 \cdot \cancel{9}}{\cancel{9} \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot \cancel{7}}$$36 = 4 \cdot 9$, $27 = 9 \cdot 3$ and $35 = 5 \cdot 7$. Divide out the $7$ and the $9$.
$=$$\dfrac{4}{15}$
Problem 2

Try problem 2 again on your own first, using problem 1 as your guide. Then compare.

$-\dfrac{5}{14} \div \left(-\dfrac{15}{28}\right)$The problem.
$=$$-\dfrac{5}{14} \cdot \left(-\dfrac{28}{15}\right)$Multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction.
$=$$\dfrac{5}{14} \cdot \dfrac{28}{15}$Both are negative, so the product is positive.
$=$$\dfrac{5 \cdot 28}{14 \cdot 15}$Multiply the numerators and the denominators.
$=$$\dfrac{\cancel{5} \cdot 2 \cdot \cancel{14}}{\cancel{14} \cdot 3 \cdot \cancel{5}}$$28 = 2 \cdot 14$ and $15 = 3 \cdot 5$. Divide out the $5$ and the $14$.
$=$$\dfrac{2}{3}$
