Multiply a Negative Fraction and a Negative Term

Try It 1.25

Your turn: problems like the one in the video
  1. Multiply: $\dfrac{11}{3}(-9a)$.
  2. Multiply: $\dfrac{13}{7}(-14b)$.
Show a hint
Decide the sign first. Write the whole number over $1$, then multiply and divide out common factors.
Show the answers

  1. $-33a$

    $-\dfrac{11 \cdot 9a}{3 \cdot 1} = -\dfrac{11 \cdot 3 \cdot 3a}{3} = -33a$.

  2. $-26b$

    $-\dfrac{13 \cdot 14b}{7 \cdot 1} = -\dfrac{13 \cdot 2 \cdot 7b}{7} = -26b$.

Show the worked solution

The same steps as the video. Each ▶ button plays the part of the video where that step is explained.

Problem 1

$\dfrac{11}{3}(-9a)$The problem. The signs are different, so the product is negative.
$=$$-\dfrac{11}{3} \cdot \dfrac{9a}{1}$Write $9a$ over $1$, with the negative sign in front.
$=$$-\dfrac{11 \cdot 9a}{3 \cdot 1}$Multiply the numerators and the denominators.
$=$$-\dfrac{11 \cdot \cancel{3} \cdot 3a}{\cancel{3} \cdot 1}$$9 = 3 \cdot 3$, so divide out a $3$.
$=$$-33a$$11 \cdot 3 = 33$.
Problem 2

Try problem 2 again on your own first, using problem 1 as your guide. Then compare.

$\dfrac{13}{7}(-14b)$The problem. The signs are different, so the product is negative.
$=$$-\dfrac{13}{7} \cdot \dfrac{14b}{1}$Write $14b$ over $1$.
$=$$-\dfrac{13 \cdot 14b}{7 \cdot 1}$Multiply the numerators and the denominators.
$=$$-\dfrac{13 \cdot 2 \cdot \cancel{7}b}{\cancel{7} \cdot 1}$$14 = 2 \cdot 7$, so divide out the $7$.
$=$$-26b$$13 \cdot 2 = 26$.
