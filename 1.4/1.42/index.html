Try It 1.42

Your turn: problems like the one in the video
  1. Given the numbers $-3$, $-\sqrt{2}$, $0.\overline{3}$, $\dfrac{9}{5}$, $4$, $\sqrt{49}$, list the ⓐ whole numbers ⓑ integers ⓒ rational numbers ⓓ irrational numbers ⓔ real numbers.
  2. Given the numbers $-\sqrt{25}$, $-\dfrac{3}{8}$, $-1$, $6$, $\sqrt{121}$, $2.041975\ldots$, list the ⓐ whole numbers ⓑ integers ⓒ rational numbers ⓓ irrational numbers ⓔ real numbers.
Show a hint
Simplify the square roots first ($\sqrt{49} = 7$). A repeating decimal is rational; a decimal that never repeats or stops is irrational. Every one of them is real.
Show the answers

  1. ⓐ $4$, $\sqrt{49}$ ⓑ $-3$, $4$, $\sqrt{49}$ ⓒ $-3$, $0.\overline{3}$, $\dfrac{9}{5}$, $4$, $\sqrt{49}$ ⓓ $-\sqrt{2}$ ⓔ all six numbers

  2. ⓐ $6$, $\sqrt{121}$ ⓑ $-\sqrt{25}$, $-1$, $6$, $\sqrt{121}$ ⓒ $-\sqrt{25}$, $-\dfrac{3}{8}$, $-1$, $6$, $\sqrt{121}$ ⓓ $2.041975\ldots$ ⓔ all six numbers

Problem 1

Simplify first: $\sqrt{49} = 7$. $0.\overline{3}$ repeats, so it is rational. $2$ is not a perfect square, so $-\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.
ⓐ Whole numbers are $0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$: $4$ and $\sqrt{49}$.
ⓑ Integers are the whole numbers and their opposites: $-3$, $4$ and $\sqrt{49}$.
ⓒ Rational numbers include every integer, every fraction and every repeating decimal: $-3$, $0.\overline{3}$, $\frac{9}{5}$, $4$ and $\sqrt{49}$.
ⓓ Irrational numbers: $-\sqrt{2}$ only.
ⓔ Real numbers: all six.
Problem 2

Try problem 2 again on your own first, using problem 1 as your guide. Then compare.

Simplify first: $-\sqrt{25} = -5$ and $\sqrt{121} = 11$. $2.041975\ldots$ never stops or repeats, so it is irrational.
ⓐ Whole numbers: $6$ and $\sqrt{121}$.
ⓑ Integers: $-\sqrt{25}$, $-1$, $6$ and $\sqrt{121}$.
ⓒ Rational numbers: $-\sqrt{25}$, $-\frac{3}{8}$, $-1$, $6$ and $\sqrt{121}$.
ⓓ Irrational numbers: $2.041975\ldots$ only.
ⓔ Real numbers: all six.
