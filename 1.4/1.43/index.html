Try It 1.43

Your turn: problems like the one in the video
  1. Locate and label the following on a number line: $-1$, $\dfrac{1}{3}$, $\dfrac{6}{5}$, $-\dfrac{7}{4}$, $\dfrac{9}{2}$, $5$, $-\dfrac{8}{3}$.
  2. Locate and label the following on a number line: $-2$, $\dfrac{2}{3}$, $\dfrac{7}{5}$, $-\dfrac{7}{4}$, $\dfrac{7}{2}$, $3$, $-\dfrac{7}{3}$.
Show a hint
Change each improper fraction to a mixed number to see which two integers it lies between.
Show the answers

  1. From left to right: $-\dfrac{8}{3} = -2\dfrac{2}{3}$, $-\dfrac{7}{4} = -1\dfrac{3}{4}$, $-1$, $\dfrac{1}{3}$, $\dfrac{6}{5} = 1\dfrac{1}{5}$, $\dfrac{9}{2} = 4\dfrac{1}{2}$, $5$

  2. From left to right: $-\dfrac{7}{3} = -2\dfrac{1}{3}$, $-2$, $-\dfrac{7}{4} = -1\dfrac{3}{4}$, $\dfrac{2}{3}$, $\dfrac{7}{5} = 1\dfrac{2}{5}$, $3$, $\dfrac{7}{2} = 3\dfrac{1}{2}$

Show the worked solution

The same steps as the video. Each ▶ button plays the part of the video where that step is explained.

Problem 1

The integers $-1$ and $5$ go right on their tick marks.
$\frac{1}{3}$ is between $0$ and $1$: split that unit into three equal parts and count one to the right of $0$.
$\frac{6}{5} = 1\frac{1}{5}$, so it is one fifth of the way from $1$ to $2$.
$-\frac{7}{4} = -1\frac{3}{4}$, so it is three fourths of the way from $-1$ to $-2$.
$\frac{9}{2} = 4\frac{1}{2}$, so it is halfway between $4$ and $5$.
$-\frac{8}{3} = -2\frac{2}{3}$, so it is two thirds of the way from $-2$ to $-3$.
From left to right: $-\frac{8}{3}$, $-\frac{7}{4}$, $-1$, $\frac{1}{3}$, $\frac{6}{5}$, $\frac{9}{2}$, $5$.
Problem 2

Try problem 2 again on your own first, using problem 1 as your guide. Then compare.

The integers $-2$ and $3$ go right on their tick marks.
$\frac{2}{3}$ is between $0$ and $1$: split that unit into three equal parts and count two to the right of $0$.
$\frac{7}{5} = 1\frac{2}{5}$, so it is two fifths of the way from $1$ to $2$.
$-\frac{7}{4} = -1\frac{3}{4}$, so it is three fourths of the way from $-1$ to $-2$.
$\frac{7}{2} = 3\frac{1}{2}$, so it is halfway between $3$ and $4$.
$-\frac{7}{3} = -2\frac{1}{3}$, so it is one third of the way from $-2$ to $-3$.
From left to right: $-\frac{7}{3}$, $-2$, $-\frac{7}{4}$, $\frac{2}{3}$, $\frac{7}{5}$, $3$, $\frac{7}{2}$.
