Find Two Numbers from Their Sum

Try It 2.16

Your turn: problems like the one in the video
  1. The sum of two numbers is negative twenty-three. One number is seven less than the other. Find the numbers.
  2. The sum of two numbers is negative eighteen. One number is forty more than the other. Find the numbers.
Show a hint
Call one number $n$ and write the other with $n$: "seven less than" $n$ is $n - 7$.
Show the answers

  1. $-15$, $-8$

  2. $-29$, $11$

Show the worked solution

The same steps as the video. Each ▶ button plays the part of the video where that step is explained.

Problem 1

Let $n$ = the first number. Seven less than it is $n - 7$, the second number.
$n + n - 7$$=$$-23$Translate: the first number plus the second number is $-23$.
$2n - 7$$=$$-23$Combine like terms.
$2n - 7 \hl{{} + 7}$$=$$-23 \hl{{} + 7}$To undo subtracting $7$, add $7$ to both sides.
$2n$$=$$-16$Simplify.
$\dfrac{2n}{\hl{2}}$$=$$\dfrac{-16}{\hl{2}}$Divide both sides by $2$.
$n$$=$$-8$Simplify. This is the first number.
The second number is $n - 7 = -8 - 7 = -15$.
The numbers are $-8$ and $-15$.

Check: $-15$ is seven less than $-8$, and $-8 + (-15) = -23$.

Problem 2

Try problem 2 again on your own first, using problem 1 as your guide. Then compare.

Let $n$ = the first number. Forty more than it is $n + 40$, the second number.
$n + n + 40$$=$$-18$Translate: the first number plus the second number is $-18$.
$2n + 40$$=$$-18$Combine like terms.
$2n + 40 \hl{{} - 40}$$=$$-18 \hl{{} - 40}$To undo adding $40$, subtract $40$ from both sides.
$2n$$=$$-58$Simplify.
$\dfrac{2n}{\hl{2}}$$=$$\dfrac{-58}{\hl{2}}$Divide both sides by $2$.
$n$$=$$-29$Simplify. This is the first number.
The second number is $n + 40 = -29 + 40 = 11$.
The numbers are $-29$ and $11$.

Check: $11$ is forty more than $-29$, and $-29 + 11 = -18$.
